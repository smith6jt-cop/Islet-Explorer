# Hypotheses that extend Barlow: conceptual revision

**29 October update:** The [current revision with full data and app-analysis coverage](revised_hypotheses_app_coverage_20261029.md) retains and sharpens R1–R6, adds R7–R11, and incorporates the subsequent marker, typing and structure decisions. 27 October 2026. Discussion proposal, not an approved execution specification or a report of findings. Prepared while tissue geometry and cell typing are being repaired in other sessions. The original H1-H10 documents and historical results remain unchanged.

## Central question

Which local tissue configurations explain why comparable inflammation accompanies different degrees of immune access to endocrine cells, and how do these relationships differ among ND, Aab-positive, and recent-onset T1D donors?

The proposed advance is to resolve the macrophage populations and physical interfaces associated with those differences. Barlow-style composition states, pseudotime, and neighborhood maps provide the common reference. Rediscovering an inflammation/beta-cell-depletion gradient would establish context; it would not be the main discovery claim.

All statements below are hypotheses or proposed comparisons. No previous numerical result is used to select a favorable hypothesis or direction.

## What Barlow already established or examined

The version of record is Barlow et al., *The extra-islet pancreas supports autoimmunity in human type 1 diabetes*, eLife, 2025, PMID 40232951, DOI 10.7554/eLife.100535.3. The local 28-page PDF, its panel tables, relevant figures, methods, and the complete PMC body were reviewed.

The study already examined islet and extra-islet immune composition, core/peri composition-based PAGA and pseudotime, CD8 functional sub-states, distance-dependent immune profiles, lobular coordination, extra-islet neural and vascular abundance, vascular IDO, and lymphoid neighborhood assemblies. A revised proposal cannot present any of these broad subjects as unexamined.

Useful openings are narrower:

## Proposed core hypotheses

These six questions form a linked program. R1-R2 concern local immune access; R3-R4 address the surrounding structural setting; R5-R6 ask how endocrine organization and object size change those relationships. The numbering is deliberately separate from the historical H1-H10 specifications.

### R1. Macrophage phenotype and position explain immune access beyond total macrophage abundance

**Hypothesis.** At comparable local immune and macrophage abundance, LGALS3-positive macrophage profiles are more concentrated at endocrine interfaces with core-localized T cells than at interfaces where T cells are confined to the surrounding tissue. CD68/CD163 background and CD4 status may distinguish these relationships.

**Why this extends the literature.** This directly addresses Barlow's unresolved myeloid heterogeneity. Macrophage-T-cell interactions themselves are not new: Steenbuck et al. 2026 already report such interactions in early disease. The increment is a specified marker profile, its position relative to a physical interface, and its association with access after accounting for the number of macrophages available.

**Discriminating comparison.** Within donors, compare endocrine objects or boundary segments with similar T-cell exposure, beta-cell availability and size. Measure macrophage-profile density separately in the core, immediate peri region and a defined outer field. Test core T-cell localization against the local macrophage profile, with absolute densities accompanying fractions. Preselect LGALS3 status as the first contrast; CD4 and CD68/CD163 combinations are refinements, not twelve simultaneous primary discoveries. Keep macrophage identities exclusive.

**Useful outcomes.** A profile-specific relationship would support information lost by pooling myeloid cells. A sufficiently precise absence would favor total burden or measured anatomy over these particular profiles. An opposite relationship would motivate testing preferential localization around retained endocrine tissue. None establishes macrophage function, M1/M2 polarization, antigen presentation, or causality.

**Clear figure.** Matched tissue examples followed by actual macrophage and T-cell densities across the boundary, with donor comparisons separated by disease.

### R2. The location and anatomical context of ColIV discontinuities matter more than average ColIV coverage

**Hypothesis.** At comparable surrounding inflammation and overall ColIV coverage, core immune localization is concentrated near particular combinations of a boundary discontinuity, a blood-vessel intersection and a macrophage-rich interface.

**Why this extends the literature.** Korpos et al. 2013 already linked basement-membrane loss at penetration sites to leukocytes and macrophage-associated proteases. A generic “collagen is protective” claim would repeat that work. Here the question is whether vascular-associated and nonvascular discontinuities have different immune configurations across ND, Aab-positive and recent-onset T1D tissue, and whether R1 profiles distinguish them.

**Discriminating comparison.** Within the same endocrine object, compare covered boundary segments, discontinuities near vessel intersections and discontinuities away from vessels, where sufficient examples exist. Account for the immune cells immediately outside each segment, segment length, object size and vascular abundance. Keep full-object summaries as a companion. This tests whether interface configuration adds information beyond total stain or total inflammation.

**Useful outcomes.** Enrichment at one configuration would prioritize a particular anatomical association for follow-up. Similar enrichment at all discontinuities would favor general discontinuity over vascular specificity. No local enrichment with adequate precision would weaken this measured route hypothesis. A gap in 2D ColIV staining does not by itself prove a physical breach or establish whether immune activity preceded it.

**Clear figure.** An image with labeled boundary segments and a direct comparison of immune density immediately inside and outside each segment category.

### R3. GAP43-associated neural configurations identify immune niches beyond nerve density

**Hypothesis.** Among regions with comparable neural abundance, GAP43-positive portions of B3TUBB-defined neural structures have different macrophage/T-cell proximity and vessel relationships from GAP43-negative portions.

**Why this extends the literature.** Barlow already associated extra-islet nerves and vessels with regional islet states. Richardson et al. 2023 already showed persistence of pancreatic nerves and capillaries despite beta-cell loss. The additional question concerns neural configuration and a plasticity-associated marker, using channels absent from Barlow's standard panel.

**Discriminating comparison.** First compare immune localization around GAP43-positive versus GAP43-negative neural segments while accounting for total neural extent, vascular proximity and regional immune burden. Then ask whether the relationship differs across endocrine composition states and disease groups. Beta-cell presence/quantity is a secondary relationship. Use accepted neural geometry; PGP9.5 alone must not turn beta-cell staining into apparent innervation. Report tissue-scale quantities as well as quantities normalized to endocrine area so object shrinkage cannot alone explain an apparent density increase.

**Useful outcomes.** A difference beyond total neural extent would distinguish a specific neural configuration from generalized nerve abundance. An association explained entirely by neural abundance or available tissue area would narrow the claim accordingly. GAP43 association is not proof of new nerve growth, functional innervation, repair, or a protective effect.

**Clear figure.** Neural/vessel/immune overlays and immune distance-to-nerve profiles split by GAP43 status and disease, using common distance axes.

### R4. Blood-vessel and lymphatic relationships distinguish different peri-islet immune organizations

**Hypothesis.** The relative placement of blood vessels and accepted lymphatic structures is associated with whether T cells and macrophage profiles are concentrated outside endocrine objects or localized in their cores, beyond the amount of either structure.

**Why this extends the literature.** Barlow measured lymphatics and vascular neighborhoods. Human lymphatic positioning near the islet-exocrine interface is also established, and extra-islet vascular/lymphatic pathology has been studied mechanistically in mice. The increment is a quantitative comparison of these two structural relationships, together with myeloid identity, in this human early-disease cohort.

**Discriminating comparison.** Compare blood-vessel-associated, lymphatic-associated and overlapping local neighborhoods with similar total immune exposure and endocrine size/composition. Measure the profile-specific outside/inside immune distribution rather than equating structure area with cell access. Use the established reviewed structure definitions; PDPN positivity alone is insufficient to label every structure a lymphatic vessel.

**Useful outcomes.** Different immune configurations would distinguish two anatomical contexts that bulk vascular density combines. Similar configurations would support a common inflammatory-field explanation within measurement precision. Static sections cannot establish ingress, egress, drainage, migration direction or lymphatic function.

**Clear figure.** A tissue map identifying both vessel classes, followed by core/peri/outer-field immune densities for each local configuration.

### R5. Endocrine-cell arrangement changes beta-cell exposure to immune cells at the same composition

**Hypothesis.** Among similarly sized endocrine objects with comparable beta/alpha/delta counts and local immune burden, objects with more beta-cell perimeter adjacent to alpha or delta cells have less beta-cell perimeter facing immune cells or the external object boundary.

**Why this extends the literature.** Barlow's endocrine composition and neighborhood analyses do not answer this particular interface question. Broader endocrine architectural variation is already described by van der Heide et al. 2026. The extension is a conditional measurement of immune exposure beyond composition, separating alpha and delta relationships.

**Discriminating comparison.** Quantify beta-alpha and beta-delta boundary contacts, beta-cell exposure at the object perimeter, and immune-beta contacts or near contacts. Use the existing fixed-geometry, fixed-composition permutation approach as a reference for expected adjacency. Account for beta-cell opportunities for contact; a higher number of beta cells mechanically creates more potential contacts. Define contact as an observed 2D spatial relation.

**Useful outcomes.** A relationship beyond composition would show that arrangement adds information about physical exposure. A precise absence would favor composition and immune burden as sufficient for this endpoint. It would not test endocrine paracrine function or establish that a particular arrangement protected cells from earlier killing.

**Clear figure.** Two objects with similar endocrine composition but different arrangements, followed by exposed beta-cell perimeter versus alpha/delta contact, with disease shown explicitly.

### R6. Small beta-bearing clusters show a different relationship to their local tissue setting than larger islets

**Hypothesis.** The density of small beta-bearing clusters changes more strongly with adverse local immune/interface configurations than the density of larger endocrine objects, with different relationships across ND, Aab-positive and recent-onset T1D donors.

**Why this extends the literature.** Barlow's ten-total-cell threshold left this part of the census outside its islet analysis. Murrall et al. 2025 already demonstrated scarcity of small beta-rich objects in T1D. The new question is which local configurations distinguish the distribution of small objects from that of larger objects, rather than another small-object count comparison.

**Discriminating comparison.** Use tissue regions with and without endocrine objects, count small beta-bearing clusters per measured tissue area and compare their association with local immune burden, ColIV organization and neural/vascular setting against larger-object density. Retain absolute counts and comparable detection support. Examine the smaller objects omitted from the embedding explicitly; do not use embedded objects as the full endocrine population. Singles remain limited to the previously authorized counts/count densities.

**Useful outcomes.** A size-dependent local relationship would identify a niche association that a conventional islet analysis misses. A similar relationship across sizes would favor a broader field effect. Comparable small-cluster scarcity across measured settings would weaken the proposed niche specificity. Cross-sectional scarcity cannot distinguish prior destruction from developmental differences, sectioning or failure to detect an object.

**Clear figure.** Whole-section maps including small clusters, plus donor-level small- and large-object densities across the same measured tissue settings.

## Two focused extensions, not additional headline claims

**Lineage-specific cycling.** Once the corrected binary Ki67 calls are available, ask whether Ki67-positive cells in each proposed niche are predominantly immune, endocrine or other cells, using each lineage's callable cells as its denominator. Barlow acquired Ki67, so marker availability is not the novelty. The extension is the relationship of cycling to the specific structural/myeloid configurations above. Immune cycling and endocrine cycling answer different questions; neither demonstrates replacement of lost beta cells or antigen-specific clonal expansion. Sparse endocrine events may support only descriptive reporting.

**Clinical relevance.** Relate one or two prespecified donor summaries from the core hypotheses to recorded C-peptide, with beta-cell quantity and relevant clinical context shown. Treat this as exploratory because there are 20 donors and a censored value. Do not manufacture a composite “support score” to obtain an association, select features on all donors before a validation split, or substitute quantitative CODEX INS for clinical C-peptide.

## Physiological interpretation of the four-marker macrophage profiles

Added in response to the owner's conceptual question on 27 October. The four binary markers define 16 mathematical profiles. The current operational macrophage rule requires CD68 or CD163, leaving 12 eligible profiles after lineage exclusions. These are measured phenotypes, not 12 established physiological cell types. Negative means below the measurement's positivity threshold, not absence of a biological pathway.

- **CD68** is predominantly an endosomal/lysosomal glycoprotein enriched in mononuclear phagocytes. Its presence supports a phagocyte phenotype but does not measure phagocytic flux or distinguish inflammatory from reparative cells. CD68-negative staining does not establish that a CD163-positive cell lacks lysosomes or cannot phagocytose. [Primary molecular characterization](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7680921/).
- **CD163** is a receptor that internalizes hemoglobin-haptoglobin complexes. This gives a concrete scavenging/iron-handling rationale, but expression alone does not show that this substrate is present or being ingested. CD163-positive does not imply universally anti-inflammatory behavior. Inflammation can also induce receptor shedding, so reduced detectable CD163 need not mark a separate lineage. [Receptor identification](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11196644/), [inflammation-associated shedding](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24275664/).
- **LGALS3/galectin-3** can participate in phagocytosis, injury responses, matrix remodeling and cell communication. Its functional consequences depend on tissue, cellular source and intracellular/extracellular location. Experimental studies support protective clearance in retinal microglia and profibrotic effects of macrophage-derived galectin-3 in diabetic kidney disease. Neither establishes the function of a galectin-3-positive pancreatic macrophage. Protein staining does not establish secretion. [Retinal study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38289348/), [kidney study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40799164/).
- **CD4** is expressed by some monocytes/macrophages as well as T cells. Its interpretation here is less specific: it can separate myeloid differentiation/maintenance states in particular tissues, but it does not confer a helper-T-cell identity or demonstrate antigen presentation. Mouse intestinal work used CD4 together with Tim-4 to distinguish populations with different replenishment rates; it does not validate resident-versus-recruited labels in human pancreas. [Mouse intestinal macrophages](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29789388/), [human monocyte CD4 internalization during culture](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10702490/).

For R1, the useful contrasts therefore hold the other markers fixed: LGALS3-positive versus negative within a CD68/CD163/CD4 background; CD163-positive versus negative within CD68-positive macrophages; and CD4-positive versus negative within the same CD68/CD163/LGALS3 background. The complete profiles remain exclusive for counting. The analysis asks whether these features distinguish spatial roles; it does not assign damaging, protective, resident, recruited or M1/M2 functions before observing the relevant evidence.

Candidate competing explanations for an interface-associated LGALS3-positive population include participation in injury/remodeling, a response to existing debris, or preferential occupation of a retained tissue niche. Its association with immune localization and structural organization can help discriminate predictions, but the four-marker profile alone cannot settle those explanations.

## How the existing app should organize this work

1. Use a single accepted saved endocrine-composition UMAP/Leiden representation and the corresponding accepted app pseudotime as a common descriptive map after the data repair. The map must belong to the corrected data version; this note does not authorize applying stale coordinates to changed object identities.
2. For R1-R4, compare measured structural/myeloid configurations among objects with similar composition where that comparison is appropriate. This asks what the composition map leaves unexplained.
3. Also examine nearby objects in the same tissue region that differ in immune localization. They share more local context than distant objects, making their measured interface differences informative. Do not call unannotated regions anatomical lobules.
4. Show the underlying physical relationship in ordinary image panels, scatterplots or distance profiles before showing model summaries. State separately which comparisons use core, immediate peri and outer tissue. A covariate is not a displayed relationship.
5. Show ND, Aab-positive and T1D directly in each hypothesis. Prespecify actual between-group contrasts when testing differences; “significant in one group but not another” is not a group-difference test. Donors remain the biological replicates.
6. The seven Aab-positive donors are useful for asking whether a configuration is present without diagnosed diabetes. They do not demonstrate that a donor will progress, assign clinical stage 1/2, or establish a temporal sequence. Single- versus multiple-autoantibody comparisons remain secondary with only three and four donors.
7. Pseudotime incorporates composition and is not elapsed time. A beta-defined composition cluster cannot independently validate a beta-quantity result. For independent tests, use the measured endpoint directly and account for shared inputs; do not indiscriminately adjust beta outcomes for beta-defined clusters.
8. Quantitative INS intensity remains excluded. Structural area, structural-cell identity, passthrough fractions and cell counts remain distinct measurements. Hormone-negative CHGA-positive cells do not establish former beta cells. No new exhausted-T-cell, Treg, IDO or antigen-specific mechanism is inferred from markers this panel lacks.

Suggested first conceptual priorities are R1, R2 and R3: they most directly exploit an explicit unresolved problem, more specific interface measurements, and additional neural markers. R4-R6 broaden the resulting biological account. This is a prioritization proposal, not an instruction to begin analyses or a judgment about existing results.

An informative null requires adequate precision. A nonsignificant result alone cannot select an alternative mechanism. Effect sizes, donor support and uncertainty should be shown without inventing a meaningful-effect threshold after seeing the results.
